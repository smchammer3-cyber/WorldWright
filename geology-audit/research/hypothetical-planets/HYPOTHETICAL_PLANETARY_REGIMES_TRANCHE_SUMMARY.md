## What this tranche changes

WorldWright must not treat planets as visual presets such as:

```text
Earthlike
Venus-like
Mars-like
Io-like
waterworld
super-Earth
```

Instead, those descriptions must emerge from:

```text
foundational planetary variables
→ thermal and mechanical state
→ base lid regime
→ water, atmosphere, tidal, gravity, rotation, ice, and impact overlays
→ geological history
→ present visible surface
```

## Base regimes now defined

- mobile lid / plate tectonics;
- stagnant lid;
- episodic lid / overturn;
- deformable, squishy, or plutonic lid;
- heat-pipe volcanic burial;
- cooling/contraction-dominated late-stage tectonics.

Each now has:

- evidence class;
- causal behavior;
- visible consequences;
- required generator state;
- minimum acceptable approximation;
- prohibited shortcuts;
- transition and inheritance rules.

## Major overlays now defined

- shallow-ocean, drowned-continent, and deep-waterworld states;
- dry-land and low-water states;
- ice-shell ocean worlds and cryovolcanism;
- modest to extreme tidal heating;
- dense-hot, thin, and airless atmospheres;
- rapid rotation, high obliquity, and tidal locking;
- high- and low-gravity worlds;
- impact-flux and geological-age states.

These overlays modify a base regime rather than replacing it.

## Confidence framework

Every hypothetical rule must be labeled:

- `OBSERVED`;
- `STRONGLY INFERRED`;
- `MODEL-SUPPORTED`;
- `CONSTRAINED EXTRAPOLATION`;
- `SPECULATIVE`;
- `FORBIDDEN OR CONTRADICTORY`.

This prevents uncertain super-Earth or Venus models from being treated as equally certain to Earth plate tectonics or Io’s observed resurfacing.

## Major scientific conclusions

### 1. History is state

Two planets with similar current mass, heat, water, and atmosphere may look different because of:

- prior lid regimes;
- overturn events;
- water loss or gain;
- burial and resurfacing;
- impact chronology;
- crust formation and preservation.

### 2. Planet style is emergent

A Venus-like, Io-like, drowned, or super-Earth world should be the result of solved variables and history, not a style label driving terrain.

### 3. Mass does not uniquely select tectonics

Competing super-Earth models support mobile, stagnant, episodic, or hemispheric outcomes depending on rheology, water, temperature, pressure, and history.

### 4. Waterworld logic requires freeboard and pressure

Deep oceans modify:

- exposed continental area;
- sediment supply;
- ocean-basin occupancy;
- seafloor pressure;
- magmatic eruption and outgassing;
- climate and interior exchange.

### 5. Heat-pipe logic requires burial

Extreme volcanism without vertical burial, surface-age reset, and crater erasure is not a heat-pipe world.

### 6. Ice-shell worlds require different mechanics

Ice thickness, brittle–ductile behavior, tidal stress, relaxation, subsurface ocean state, and cryovolcanism must replace ordinary rocky tectonic and fluvial assumptions.

### 7. Crater retention must reconcile with resurfacing

A young crater-poor surface requires volcanism, burial, deformation, erosion, relaxation, or another explicit erasure process.

Future WorldWright architecture must carry:

- prior regimes and transition events;
- resurfacing, burial, impact, and exposure ages;
- overlay state and strength;
- terrain provenance;
- evidence/confidence class;
- contradiction rules;
- material, heat, water, and age reconciliation.
