# Ocean margins, structural bathymetry, and sea-level source register

## Scope

This register supports Stage 1 research for:

- oceanic crust age, cooling, and thermal subsidence;
- ridge flanks, fracture zones, abyssal hills, and trenches;
- continental shelves, slopes, rises, and rifted margins;
- submarine canyons, channels, fans, and mass wasting;
- abyssal plains, sediment cover, contour currents, and deep-ocean deposition;
- oceanic plateaus, seamounts, volcanic rises, and dynamic support;
- water inventory, basin hypsometry, connectivity, sea level, and inundation;
- generator obligations for basement, sediment, water surface, depth, provenance, and mass/volume balance.

The evidence-state labels follow `source-register.md`.

**Every source in this file is citation-only during Stage 1. No map, bathymetric grid, seismic line, figure, photograph, or dataset is approved for Stage 2 use without a separate asset-specific licensing decision.**

## Source-use rules established by this tranche

1. **Feature identity is not depth class.** Ridge, trench, shelf, slope, rise, plateau, and plain must derive from geological cause.
2. **Ocean floor has layers.** Basement elevation, sediment/deposit thickness, water surface, and water depth must remain separate.
3. **Ocean age comes from spreading history.** It cannot be radial distance from a basin center or inferred canonically from depth.
4. **Thermal subsidence is a baseline, not the whole basin.** Sediment, volcanism, flexure, dynamic support, and deformation create departures.
5. **Margins are inherited crustal systems.** The current coastline cannot generate the shelf or margin architecture.
6. **Shelf dimensions are not universal.** Crust, sediment, tectonics, glaciation, reefs, volcanism, and sea-level history all matter.
7. **Canyons and fans are source-to-sink systems.** They require sediment source, pathway, receiving sink, and activity state.
8. **Highstand deep-water delivery is possible.** Sea level changes connectivity but does not globally switch deposition on/off.
9. **Abyssal plains are depositional surfaces.** Smoothness must emerge through sediment burial relative to basement relief.
10. **Water level is a volume/topology solution.** Land fraction is derived; isolated basins require spill and connectivity logic.
11. **Basin-volume effects must not be double counted.** Ridge, sediment, dynamic, and volcanic changes alter solid-surface capacity before the water solve.
12. **Every source remains citation-only until asset review.** Scientific use does not establish redistribution permission.
