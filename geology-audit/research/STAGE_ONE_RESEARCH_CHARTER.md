# Stage 1 geological research charter

## Mission

Stage 1 builds the scientific and visual specification that Stage 2 will later use to generate a large, indexed reference-image library.

**No generated reference imagery is permitted during Stage 1.**

Stage 1 asks:

1. What physical circumstances can a rocky world have?
2. Which geological processes follow from those circumstances?
3. What visible consequences should those processes produce?
4. How do those consequences change across thresholds, age, climate, gravity, and scale?
5. Which unusual outcomes are valid exceptions?
6. Which procedural artifacts can imitate geology while violating the causal rules?
7. Exactly which reference cases must Stage 2 eventually generate?
8. What must WorldWright know, preserve, route, and transmit to produce those outcomes causally?

## Two-stage gate

### Stage 1 — Research and specification

Outputs:
- research taxonomy;
- authoritative source register;
- causal process briefs;
- threshold and interaction matrices;
- multiscale visual-obligation records;
- generator-obligation records;
- exception and ambiguity catalog;
- procedural-failure catalog;
- Stage 2 coverage matrix;
- labeling, provenance, and licensing requirements.

### Stage 2 — Reference generation and curation

Stage 2 may begin only after:
- every required Stage 1 domain has passed its completion checklist;
- the coverage matrix contains no unexplained gaps;
- unresolved scientific uncertainties are explicitly marked;
- generator obligations have been reconciled across domains;
- the user explicitly approves moving to generation.

## Research principles

### 1. Causes before appearances

The research must begin from physical causes and process relationships, not from visual resemblance.

```text
planetary circumstances
→ geodynamic regime
→ crustal and tectonic causes
→ explicit geological features
→ topography and bathymetry
→ erosion, sedimentation, hydrology, and climate modification
→ final visible surface
```

### 2. No single Earth analogue is universal

Earth examples are evidence for process families, not mandatory templates. Each process must be represented by multiple valid morphologies, ages, climates, and scales.

### 3. Relationships matter more than exact dimensions

Stage 1 should prefer rules such as:
- ridge follows spreading system;
- trench and arc remain causally paired;
- foreland depression lies adjacent to orogenic loading;
- inundation expands through connected low terrain;

rather than unsupported universal constants.

### 4. Uncertainty is part of the specification

When sources or models disagree, Stage 1 must record:
- the competing interpretations;
- the evidence supporting each;
- which parts can safely become visual obligations;
- which parts must remain broad scenario branches.

### 5. Generated imagery cannot become its own authority

Stage 2 imagery will be produced from the Stage 1 specification. Stage 1 rules cannot later be justified solely by Stage 2 outputs.

### 6. Visual rules require generator obligations

Every remaining domain must document both:

- what the real system does and how it appears; and
- what WorldWright must know, preserve, route, and transmit to produce it.

A domain cannot become `SPEC COMPLETE` by documenting appearance alone.

## Source hierarchy

### Tier A — Primary observational and institutional sources

Preferred for direct evidence:
- geological-survey maps and reports;
- NASA, USGS, NOAA, ESA, JAXA, and equivalent mission or data products;
- peer-reviewed observational studies;
- published digital elevation, bathymetric, gravity, and geological datasets.

### Tier B — Peer-reviewed process and modeling studies

Used for:
- causal interpretation;
- parameter sensitivity;
- geodynamic regimes;
- threshold and scaling hypotheses;
- cases that cannot be directly observed.

Model results must be labeled as model-dependent and must not be treated as settled observation.

### Tier C — Scholarly reviews and reference works

Used to synthesize established concepts and locate primary evidence.

### Tier D — Educational summaries

May help orientation, but cannot alone establish a rule, threshold, or exception.

### Excluded as authority

- unattributed diagrams;
- SEO summaries;
- artistic planetary renders;
- procedural-generator outputs;
- uncited social posts;
- repeated secondary claims with no traceable primary basis.

## Required evidence pattern per topic

Each completed process topic should have, where available:

1. **Observed example** — a mapped or measured real case.
2. **Process explanation** — why that morphology forms.
3. **Variation examples** — at least two substantially different valid expressions.
4. **Threshold evidence** — how morphology changes with a controlling parameter.
5. **Counterexample or exception** — a similar-looking form with a different cause.
6. **Failure analogue** — how procedural generation could reproduce the shape incorrectly.
7. **Generator obligation** — what canonical state and downstream consequences are required.

## Required scales

Every process brief must describe consequences at four scales.

### Planetary scale
- global elevation distribution;
- land/ocean organization;
- dominant tectonic or resurfacing fabric;
- hemispheric or long-wavelength asymmetry.

### Continental / basin scale
- mountain and basin networks;
- margins, shelves, plateaus, rifts, arcs, and cratons;
- continent-to-ocean transitions.

### Regional scale
- individual belts, basin systems, volcanic provinces, drainage domains, and erosional regions.

### Local scale
- ridge-and-valley texture;
- scarps, canyons, faults, channels, dunes, moraines, lava flows, crater rims, and other fine expression.

A process may be visible at only some scales. That absence must also be documented.

## Required threshold structure

Each controlling variable should be researched through at least these states when meaningful:

- absent / negligible;
- low;
- moderate;
- high;
- extreme;
- post-active / inherited / fossil state.

Thresholds may be numeric, ordinal, or relational depending on evidence quality. Numeric thresholds require defensible calibration and uncertainty bounds.

## Interaction requirement

No major variable may be studied only in isolation. Stage 1 must identify high-value interactions such as:

- gravity × lithospheric strength;
- internal heat × tectonic regime;
- uplift × erosion;
- sea level × shelf topography;
- precipitation × drainage integration;
- glaciation × pre-existing mountain structure;
- crust thickness × density × isostasy;
- convergence obliquity × fault partitioning;
- volcanic supply × lithosphere thickness.

A domain is complete only when all applicable boxes are satisfied:

- [ ] scope and vocabulary defined;
- [ ] source register established;
- [ ] causal chain documented;
- [ ] planetary-scale consequences documented;
- [ ] continental/basin-scale consequences documented;
- [ ] regional consequences documented;
- [ ] local consequences documented;
- [ ] controlling parameters identified;
- [ ] threshold behavior documented;
- [ ] important parameter interactions documented;
- [ ] temporal evolution documented;
- [ ] valid morphological diversity documented;
- [ ] exceptions and ambiguous analogues documented;
- [ ] procedural failure signatures documented;
- [ ] generator obligations documented;
- [ ] Stage 2 case requirements specified;
- [ ] remaining uncertainty explicitly recorded;
- [ ] user approval recorded.

## Immediate research sequence

The Stage 1 order is deliberately causal:

1. planetary foundations and geodynamic regimes;
2. crustal architecture;
3. tectonic boundary families;
4. collision, orogeny, plateaus, collapse, and inherited belts;
5. volcanism and magmatic provinces;
6. ocean margins and structural bathymetry;
7. fluvial, erosion, weathering, and sediment systems;
8. glacial, aeolian, coastal, and karst processes;
9. impacts and resurfacing;
10. temporal, climate, and non-Earth interactions;
11. procedural failures and multiscale coherence;
12. complete Stage 2 generation matrix;
13. post-research causal-architecture reconciliation and implementation roadmap.
