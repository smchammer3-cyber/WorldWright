# Abyssal plains, deep-sea sediment, and bottom-current systems

## Research status

- **Domain IDs:** O04, linked to O03, S04–S05, T01, V03, X02–X03
- **Status:** IN RESEARCH
- **Stage 2 generation:** BLOCKED
- **Generator implementation:** DEFERRED UNTIL STAGE 1 RESEARCH COMPLETION
- **Purpose:** define deep-ocean smoothness, channels, fans, drifts, sediment waves, and exposed basement as products of sediment supply, accommodation, currents, tectonic inheritance, and time.

## 1. Central rule

An abyssal plain is not the default unstructured bottom of an ocean basin.

It is commonly a sediment-mantled deep-ocean surface where pre-existing basement relief has been partly or extensively buried.

```text
oceanic basement fabric
+ pelagic/hemipelagic sediment
+ gravity-flow deposits
+ bottom-current redistribution
+ volcanic/impact input
+ compaction and deformation
= observed deep-sea surface
```

A smooth abyssal surface may hide rough basement rather than prove that the crust was generated smooth.

## 2. Basement versus sediment surface

Deep ocean floor may contain:

- abyssal hills;
- fracture zones;
- extinct ridge structures;
- seamounts and volcanic plateaus;
- flexural depressions;
- trenches and outer rises;
- impact structures;
- fault scarps.

Sediment can:

- drape relief;
- fill lows;
- leave highs exposed;
- channelize around obstacles;
- form levees and fans;
- compact and subside;
- be reworked by bottom currents;
- fail and remobilize.

WorldWright must preserve both basement topography and depositional surface.

## 3. Sediment sources

Deep-sea sediment may originate from:

- continents and rivers;
- submarine canyons and mass wasting;
- volcanic islands and arcs;
- glacial margins;
- windblown dust;
- biogenic production;
- hydrothermal and authigenic processes;
- impact ejecta;
- reworking of older marine deposits.

Source and transport history determine material, thickness, geometry, and age.

## 4. Pelagic and hemipelagic drape

Fine particles settling through the water column can form broad drapes.

Potential controls:

- distance from continental source;
- biological productivity;
- ocean chemistry;
- dissolution depth;
- wind/dust supply;
- current winnowing;
- seafloor age;
- volcanic ash input.

Drape tends to smooth small relief gradually, but may remain thin on young, current-swept, sediment-starved, or topographically isolated seafloor.

## 5. Turbidity currents and submarine fans

Dense sediment-laden flows may move through canyons and channels into deep basins.

Potential products:

- channel–levee systems;
- lobes;
- basin-floor fans;
- sheet-like turbidites;
- erosional scours;
- avulsed channel belts;
- sediment waves;
- ponded deposits in confined basins.

Important controls:

- sediment supply;
- grain size;
- slope and confinement;
- canyon connectivity;
- sea level and coastline position;
- earthquake/storm/river triggers;
- receiving-basin geometry;
- interaction with bottom currents.

The same deep basin may contain active channels, abandoned fans, smooth drape, and exposed basement.

## 6. Contour currents and sediment drifts

Persistent deep-water currents can erode, transport, and deposit sediment along isobaths or around topographic obstacles.

Potential forms:

- contourite drifts;
- moats;
- sediment waves;
- plastered or mounded deposits;
- current-scoured gaps;
- asymmetric sediment bodies;
- channel–drift interactions.

These forms require ocean-circulation context and cannot be represented as generic directional noise.

## 7. Abyssal channels and connectivity

Deep-sea channels can extend far beyond canyon mouths and cross very low-gradient basins.

Their paths may respond to:

- subtle relief;
- inherited fracture zones;
- basin confinement;
- fan avulsion;
- volcanic highs;
- sediment deposition and self-formed levees;
- tectonic tilting;
- current interaction.

Deep channels are not simply underwater rivers copied from land. Their driving density and depositional behavior differ.

## 8. Sediment-starved basins

Some basins preserve prominent basement fabric because of:

- great distance from continents;
- young seafloor;
- low biological/dust input;
- current winnowing;
- topographic barriers;
- trench interception;
- limited glacial/fluvial supply.

Potential appearance:

- exposed abyssal hills;
- visible fracture zones;
- isolated pockets of sediment;
- volcanic mounds and scarps;
- rougher seafloor than sediment-rich basins.

## 9. Sediment-rich basins

High supply and long time may produce:

- broad smooth plains;
- buried ridge/flank fabric;
- thick fans and aprons;
- ponded basins;
- channel–levee networks;
- reduced exposed relief;
- compaction-driven subsidence;
- flexural loading.

Smoothness must emerge through deposited thickness relative to basement amplitude.

## 10. Trench and basin interception

Trenches may intercept sediment moving from continents into deep ocean.

Consequences:

- trench fill;
- axial sediment transport;
- sediment starvation beyond the trench;
- accretionary-prism growth;
- subduction of sediment;
- along-strike variability.

A deep-ocean sediment system must account for structural barriers and sinks.

## 11. Volcanic and topographic obstacles

Seamounts, plateaus, ridges, and islands may:

- divert channels;
- create sediment shadows;
- focus currents;
- collect drape on flanks;
- trigger slope failures;
- become partially buried;
- preserve exposed summits;
- generate local moats or scour.

Sediment deposition cannot ignore existing relief.

## 12. Compaction, subsidence, and loading

As sediment accumulates:

- pore space decreases;
- deposits compact;
- the basement may subside under load;
- accommodation may increase;
- fluid migration and deformation may occur;
- slope stability may change.

A simplified generator may use normalized compaction and flexural responses, but it must not treat deposited thickness as equal to original supplied volume without reconciliation.

## 13. Deep-sea temporal evolution

A basin-floor sector may pass through:

1. newly created rough oceanic crust;
2. thin drape over visible abyssal hills;
3. channel/fan initiation;
4. progressive basin fill;
5. bottom-current reworking;
6. burial of older structures;
7. tectonic tilting or trench interception;
8. deformation, subduction, or preservation in accreted sedimentary units.

The visible smoothness can therefore be much younger than the crust.

### Conservation/reconciliation

```text
sediment supplied
≈ deposited sediment
+ suspended/exported sediment
+ subducted/removed sediment
± compaction/chemical-loss approximation
```

## 17. Procedural failure signatures

### 17.1 Abyssal blur
Deep ocean is smoothed uniformly without sediment thickness or source history.

### 17.2 Sediment-from-nowhere
Plains and rises appear without upstream material.

### 17.3 Underwater-river copy
Deep channels use land-river rules and ignore density flow, levees, and deposition.

### 17.4 Fan stamp
A radial lobe is placed at every canyon mouth regardless of basin slope and supply.

### 17.5 Current texture
Directional ridges are added without a circulation/current field.

### 17.6 Basement deletion
Burial permanently erases ridge, fracture-zone, and impact history.

### 17.7 No barrier interaction
Channels and deposits pass through seamounts, trenches, and plateaus.

### 17.8 Infinite accommodation
Sediment accumulates without compaction, subsidence, spill, or routing change.

### 17.9 No mass balance
Eroded continental and volcanic material does not match marine deposition or export.

## 20. Conclusions safe enough to carry forward

### High confidence

- abyssal plains are commonly depositional surfaces over older basement relief;
- smoothness depends on sediment thickness relative to basement relief, not generic blur;
- deep-sea sediment arrives through multiple source and transport pathways;
- turbidity systems and bottom-current systems form different but interacting morphologies;
- barriers, trenches, and volcanic highs reorganize sediment routing;
- sediment mass and provenance must be conserved or explicitly exported/removed.

### Model-dependent or incomplete

- current-field simplification;
- quantitative sedimentation rates;
- channel avulsion thresholds;
- compaction/flexure coupling;
- biogenic and chemical sediment budgets across planets.

### Not approved

- uniform abyssal smoothing;
- generic fan stamps;
- underwater river copying;
- generator implementation;
- image generation.
