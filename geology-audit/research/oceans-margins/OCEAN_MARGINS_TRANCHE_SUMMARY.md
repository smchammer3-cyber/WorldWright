## Main scientific conclusions

### 1. Geological identity and water depth are separate

The required relation is:

```text
basement/crustal structure
+ tectonic, volcanic, flexural, and dynamic support
+ sediment and deposits
= solid seafloor elevation

water-surface elevation
- solid seafloor elevation
= water depth
```

A trench is not defined by being deep, and a ridge is not defined by being shallow.

### 2. Oceanic crust age derives from spreading history

Oceanic lithosphere generally cools and subsides as it moves away from ridges, but actual bathymetry also reflects sediment, volcanism, fracture zones, flexure, and mantle support.

A basin-center distance field cannot substitute for ridge ancestry and crustal age.

### 3. Shelves are inherited margins, not coast-distance halos

Shelf width, break, slope, and rise architecture depend on:

- continental/transitional/oceanic crust geometry;
- rift and breakup history;
- thermal subsidence;
- active-margin tectonics;
- sediment supply and compaction;
- glaciation, reefs, and volcanism;
- sea-level and exposure history.

The current coastline is a temporary intersection between the solid surface and water surface. It cannot create the shelf.

### 4. Submarine canyons are source-to-sink systems

Canyons may connect rivers, littoral cells, shelf edges, slope failures, glacial troughs, or tectonic weaknesses to deep-sea channels and fans.

Lowstands can increase shelf connectivity, but highstand deep-water sediment delivery is also possible where canyon and sediment pathways remain connected.

### 5. Abyssal plains are depositional surfaces

A smooth abyssal plain commonly represents sediment burial over older rough basement.

WorldWright must preserve:

- basement relief;
- sediment thickness and provenance;
- channels, fans, levees, and drifts;
- bottom-current reworking;
- compaction and loading;
- exposed versus buried structural fabric.

Smooth deep ocean cannot be generated by blurring basement terrain.

### 6. Sea level is a water-volume and topology solution

Land fraction should emerge from:

- solid-surface hypsometry;
- connected basin geometry;
- liquid-water volume;
- ice and isolated-water storage;
- uplift/subsidence and basin-capacity changes.

A target land fraction may guide scenario intent, but a quantile-selected sea level cannot remain canonical authority.

### 7. Isolated basins require spill logic

A single global threshold cannot correctly resolve inland seas and lakes.

Basin filling must account for:

- connectivity;
- sill/spill elevation;
- merging and isolation thresholds;
- water-budget transfer;
- evaporation/precipitation where enabled.

### 8. Basin-volume effects must not be double counted

Ridge volume, sediment fill, volcanic plateaus, flexure, and dynamic topography alter the solid-surface capacity of ocean basins. Sea level then responds through the water-volume solve.

The same effect must not independently change both seafloor elevation and water surface at full amplitude.

## Generator obligations discovered

Future WorldWright architecture must be able to carry at least:

- ocean-basin identity and history;
- crust type, age, thickness, density, and ridge ancestry;
- basement elevation;
- thermal-subsidence state;
- ridge, fracture-zone, abyssal-hill, trench, plateau, and seamount structures;
- sediment thickness, material, age, and provenance;
- shelf/slope/rise margin ancestry;
- canyon, channel, fan, levee, and drift networks;
- bottom-current and gravity-flow process state;
- connected-ocean and isolated-basin volumes;
- basin connectivity and spill elevations;
- water-surface elevation and true water depth;
- transgression/regression and exposure history;
- sediment and water mass/volume reconciliation.

These are research findings, not implementation authorization.
