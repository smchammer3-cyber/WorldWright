# Floodplains, alluvial fans, deltas, and avulsion

## Research status

- **Domain IDs:** S05, linked to S01–S04, O01–O05, X01–X03, H01–H06
- **Status:** IN RESEARCH
- **Stage 2 generation:** BLOCKED
- **Generator implementation:** DEFERRED UNTIL STAGE 1 RESEARCH COMPLETION
- **Purpose:** define distributary and depositional landscapes as evolving systems controlled by sediment supply, discharge, accommodation, base level, subsidence, waves/tides, channel mobility, and avulsion.

## 1. Central rule

A delta or fan is not a radial stamp.

The required system is:

```text
water / debris / sediment source
+ confinement and slope break
+ sediment supply and grain class
+ discharge variability
+ accommodation and base level
+ waves, tides, currents, vegetation, or ice where relevant
→ deposition and channel building
→ avulsion, lobe switching, abandonment, erosion, and reoccupation
→ evolving fan, floodplain, or delta
```

## 2. Floodplains

Floodplains form through repeated channel migration, overbank flooding, avulsion, levee building, crevasse splays, abandoned-channel filling, and vertical accretion.

Potential components:

- active channel belt;
- natural levees;
- point bars;
- braid bars;
- oxbow lakes;
- backswamps or wetlands;
- crevasse splays;
- paleochannels;
- terraces;
- flood basins;
- floodplain lakes;
- tributary fans.

A floodplain is therefore a history of channel occupancy and sediment storage, not a uniformly flat lowland.

## 3. Channel patterns

### Meandering

Favored by combinations of:

- cohesive banks;
- moderate sediment supply;
- relatively low valley slope;
- sustained flow;
- floodplain exchange.

Potential consequences:

- lateral migration;
- point bars;
- cutoffs and oxbows;
- scroll bars;
- channel-belt deposits.

### Braided

Favored by combinations of:

- high bed-material supply;
- variable discharge;
- erodible banks;
- relatively steep slopes;
- limited stabilizing vegetation/cohesion.

Potential consequences:

- multiple shifting channels;
- bars and islands;
- rapid aggradation;
- wide channel belts.

### Anabranching/wandering

May combine multiple stable or semi-stable channels with islands and floodplain vegetation or cohesive banks.

These are tendencies, not one-variable classifications.

## 4. Avulsion

Avulsion is a rapid or progressive shift of a river or distributary to a new course.

Potential causes:

- channel-bed aggradation;
- levee superelevation;
- floodplain slope advantage;
- tectonic tilting;
- subsidence;
- delta-lobe growth;
- blockage by sediment, ice, vegetation, lava, or landslides;
- extreme floods;
- channel capture.

Potential consequences:

- abandoned channels;
- new distributary lobes;
- floodplain reworking;
- rapid sediment redistribution;
- changed wetland/lake patterns;
- local flooding;
- altered marine sediment delivery.

### Generator obligation

WorldWright needs:

- channel-belt elevation relative to floodplain;
- alternative pathway slopes;
- avulsion threshold/state;
- active and abandoned channels;
- lobe/channel residence time;
- sediment and discharge redistribution.

## 5. Alluvial fans

Alluvial fans commonly form where a confined transport system exits a steep valley or mountain front into an open lower-gradient basin.

Potential construction processes:

- streamflow deposition;
- braided distributaries;
- sheet floods;
- hyperconcentrated flows;
- debris flows;
- channel plugging and nodal avulsion;
- tectonic tilting;
- fan-head incision;
- lobe switching.

### Fan controls

- source relief and erosion;
- feeder-channel confinement;
- sediment volume and grain size;
- water/discharge regime;
- basin slope;
- accommodation/subsidence;
- tectonic uplift;
- climate and event frequency;
- infiltration and evaporation;
- neighboring-fan interaction.

### Valid radiality

Fans are a major valid radial or sectoral landform, but only when:

- an apex/source channel exists;
- slope and confinement change at the apex;
- sediment supply is explicit;
- active lobes and abandoned sectors are represented;
- grain/deposit patterns change downslope;
- the radial form is local to the depositional system.

A fan may be asymmetric, confined, coalesced, incised, dissected, or partly buried.

## 6. Bajadas and megafans

Neighboring fans may merge into a bajada along a mountain front.

Large fluvial megafans may develop where:

- a major river exits a mountain belt;
- sediment supply is sustained;
- basin accommodation is broad;
- channel avulsion distributes deposits over a wide area;
- tectonic tilting and subsidence guide lobe migration.

Megafans are not simply enlarged small debris-flow fans. Their channel dynamics and scale differ.

## 7. Deltas

Deltas form where a river delivers sediment to a standing body of water and deposition builds the shoreline basinward or vertically.

Potential components:

- delta plain;
- distributary channels;
- interdistributary bays;
- natural levees and splays;
- wetlands/lakes;
- mouth bars;
- delta front;
- prodelta;
- abandoned lobes;
- shelf-edge extensions;
- submarine channels/fans.

A delta requires both sediment delivery and accommodation. It can retreat or drown if sediment supply is insufficient relative to subsidence, compaction, erosion, or water-level rise.

## 8. River-, wave-, and tide-influenced deltas

Delta morphology reflects interacting agents.

### River-dominated tendency

- strong distributary protrusion;
- elongate lobes;
- mouth-bar growth;
- rapid local progradation;
- high fluvial sediment supply.

### Wave-influenced tendency

- sediment redistributed alongshore;
- smoother or cuspate shoreline;
- barriers and beach ridges;
- fewer dominant distributary mouths in some settings.

### Tide-influenced tendency

- funnel-shaped channels;
- tidal bars and islands;
- bidirectional flow;
- elongated channel networks;
- strong estuarine exchange.

Natural deltas commonly combine all three. A ternary “style” must not become a fixed shape template.

## 9. Delta lobe switching

A delta may alternate among lobes because of:

- avulsion;
- differential subsidence;
- channel aggradation;
- mouth-bar growth;
- shoreline geometry;
- wave/tide reworking;
- sea-level change;
- tectonic tilting;
- inherited paleovalleys.

Abandoned lobes may:

- compact and subside;
- erode;
- become wetlands or bays;
- be reoccupied;
- remain preserved offshore;
- feed barrier systems.

## 10. Backwater and low-gradient effects

Near a standing body of water, river hydraulics and sedimentation can be affected over a backwater reach.

Potential consequences:

- spatial changes in deposition;
- avulsion clustering;
- channel-width/depth adjustment;
- floodplain aggradation;
- mouth-bar formation;
- interaction with tides and storm surges.

A delta cannot be generated only by extending the lowest-elevation river cells seaward.

## 11. Delta sinking, compaction, and relative sea level

Deltas are especially sensitive to:

- sediment compaction;
- tectonic subsidence;
- sediment loading;
- groundwater/fluid withdrawal where human processes are modeled;
- reduced sediment delivery;
- relative sea-level rise;
- storm and wave erosion.

For natural WorldWright generation, the important rule is:

```text
sediment supply and land-building
must be reconciled against
subsidence + compaction + erosion + water-level change
```

## 12. Lake deltas and inland systems

Deltas can form in:

- tectonic lakes;
- volcanic/caldera lakes;
- glacial lakes;
- impact basins;
- terminal arid lakes;
- temporary dammed lakes.

Potential differences from marine deltas:

- smaller wave/tide forcing;
- rapid lake-level changes;
- strong sediment trapping;
- fan-delta transitions;
- evaporite and playa interaction;
- catastrophic breach and incision.

## 13. Fan deltas

Fan deltas occur where an alluvial fan builds directly into a standing body of water.

They combine:

- steep coarse sediment delivery;
- fan processes;
- shoreline and underwater deposition;
- tectonic or volcanic basin margins;
- mass wasting and submarine continuation.

They should remain distinct from low-gradient river deltas.

## 14. Alluvial plains and basin fills

Broad alluvial plains may form through:

- repeated river avulsion;
- floodplain aggradation;
- coalescing fans;
- axial river systems;
- lake/delta migration;
- tectonic subsidence;
- episodic incision and terrace formation.

They are not uniformly flat. They contain subtle channel belts, abandoned lobes, levees, wetlands, distributary splays, and terraces.

## 15. Planetary-regime branches

### Humid Earthlike branch

- perennial rivers;
- meandering/anabranching systems;
- extensive floodplains and wetlands;
- high sediment throughput;
- marine and lake deltas.

### Arid episodic branch

- debris-flow and flash-flood fans;
- playas and terminal deltas;
- long inactive intervals;
- channel plugging and abrupt avulsion;
- wind reworking.

### Low-gravity branch

- altered settling, channel geometry, slope, and runout;
- cohesion and fluid density may strongly affect fan/delta shape;
- Earth fan slopes and lobe dimensions cannot be copied directly.

### High-gravity branch

- altered settling and bank/slope stability;
- potentially compact lower-relief depositional forms;
- fluid properties, sediment density, and subsidence remain essential.

### Thin-atmosphere ancient Mars-like branch

- preserved fans and deltas may record episodic wet climates;
- current aridity may leave forms inactive and exposed;
- burial/exhumation and impact chronology matter;
- alluvial fans can postdate earlier river systems.

### Deep-waterworld branch

- little exposed land may limit classic continental deltas;
- island arcs, volcanic islands, glaciers, and submarine sediment systems dominate;
- drowned continental deltas may be preserved underwater;
- high water pressure and low land area alter sediment supply.

### Ice-shell/meltwater branch

- local fan or delta-like deposits may form where meltwater reaches basins;
- ordinary global river-delta assumptions are forbidden without persistent surface liquid.

## 18. Procedural failure signatures

### 18.1 Radial fan stamp
A symmetric fan appears without apex, source, slope break, or active lobes.

### 18.2 Delta icon
Every river mouth receives one triangular or bird-foot shape.

### 18.3 Static distributaries
Channels never avulse, abandon, migrate, or switch lobes.

### 18.4 Uniform floodplain
Lowlands are flattened with no channel belts, levees, splays, or lakes.

### 18.5 No accommodation
Deposition occurs without subsidence, base level, or available space.

### 18.6 No compaction or drowning
Old delta lobes remain permanently high and active.

### 18.7 One-process delta
Wave, tide, and river effects are mutually exclusive fixed templates.

### 18.8 Source-free fan/delta
A deposit appears without upstream erosion and transport.

### 18.9 Planetary template leakage
Earth delta and fan geometry is copied unchanged to exotic gravity/atmosphere/water regimes.

## 21. Conclusions safe enough to carry forward

### High confidence

- floodplains are built by repeated channel occupation, migration, flooding, and abandonment;
- avulsion is essential to fan, floodplain, and delta evolution;
- fan radiality is valid only with a source apex, slope change, sediment supply, and lobe history;
- deltas require sediment supply, accommodation, and receiving-basin processes;
- river, wave, and tide influences combine rather than form rigid templates;
- compaction, subsidence, erosion, and water-level change determine preservation.

### Model-dependent or incomplete

- universal avulsion thresholds;
- cross-planet channel geometry;
- wave/tide approximations;
- fan runout under exotic fluids;
- biological stabilization.

### Not approved

- fan/delta stamps;
- static distributaries;
- uniform lowland flattening;
- generator implementation;
- image generation.
