## Major conclusions from the remaining science

### Ice

- glaciers require mass balance, thickness, flow, basal thermal state, water, substrate, and time;
- cold-based ice can preserve terrain while warm-based/fast ice strongly erodes it;
- ice sheets selectively erode outlet corridors and preserve some interiors;
- fjords require glacial excavation followed by inundation;
- glacial sediment, meltwater, loading, and rebound must be routed and conserved;
- permafrost thaw can create new subsidence, lakes, gullies, and sediment pulses.

### Wind

- aeolian transport requires an atmosphere, threshold crossing, available sediment, grain properties, gravity, and directional history;
- dune fields are collective evolving systems rather than repeated templates;
- dust links local erosion to regional/global atmosphere and distant deposition;
- yardangs require suitable substrate and abrasive flux;
- ordinary aeolian systems are forbidden on airless surfaces.

### Coasts

- a coastline is a moving intersection, not a fixed geological boundary;
- waves, tides, storms, sediment cells, cliffs, barriers, inlets, reefs, and relative sea level interact;
- beach/barrier migration and cliff retreat require sediment budgets;
- tides depend on orbital and basin geometry;
- reefs are optional life/chemistry branches and must compete with drowning, burial, and erosion;
- shoreline dilation or fixed-width beach bands are invalid.

### Karst

- karst requires soluble material, reactive fluid, fractures, recharge, hydraulic gradients, conduits, and springs;
- surface rivers and groundwater networks must exchange water and sediment;
- sinkholes include slow solution/subsidence and rapid collapse branches;
- cave levels preserve base-level history;
- dissolved mass must enter an explicit export/precipitation ledger;
- lava tubes, thermokarst, impact pits, and tectonic fissures are lookalikes, not automatic karst.

### Impacts

- impacts require event energy, target state, excavation, transient crater, modification, ejecta, shock, melt, and later degradation;
- simple, complex, peak-ring, and multi-ring basins are different mechanical regimes;
- ejecta and displaced target mass require conservation;
- large basins modify crustal thickness, gravity, stress, volcanism, and sediment accommodation;
- crater density must reconcile with impact flux, secondaries, saturation, burial, erosion, and resurfacing;
- impact radiality is valid only with event and component provenance.

### Climate–geology coupling

- mountains modify precipitation, winds, snow, and climate through geometry and circulation;
- climate controls process mode and event distributions rather than directly painting terrain;
- erosion and deposition can feed back through isostasy, flexure, loading, and wedge mechanics;
- current climate and formation climate must remain separate;
- volcanic and impact forcing can warm or cool depending on event, composition, atmosphere, ocean, and timescale;
- rare extremes may perform most geomorphic work.

### Time and landscape memory

- material age, structure age, exposure age, and activity state are distinct;
- burial conceals but must not delete older state;
- exhumation can reveal paleochannels, craters, surfaces, and inverted relief;
- inherited faults, rifts, sutures, craters, valleys, and deposits guide later processes;
- event ordering is noncommutative;
- regime transitions leave mixed-age palimpsest landscapes.

### Multiscale and procedural integrity

- every feature needs scale ownership;
- graph/topology authority is required for plates, faults, rivers, sediment routes, coasts, ice outlets, karst conduits, and event lineage;
- subgrid state must preserve density, orientation, size, material, age, and flux—not become noise;
- staged diagnostics must locate the earliest causal failure;
- projection seams, poles, tile boundaries, and cell-area differences require explicit tests;
- visual review remains mandatory even when numerical tests pass.
