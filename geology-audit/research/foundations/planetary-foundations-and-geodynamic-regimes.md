# Planetary foundations and geodynamic regimes

## Research status

- **Domain IDs:** F01–F05, G01–G05
- **Status:** IN RESEARCH
- **Stage 2 generation:** BLOCKED
- **Purpose:** establish which global physical parameters must condition all later geological reference families.

This module comes before collision belts, rifts, shelves, rivers, or visual scoring. A mountain system cannot be judged correctly unless the audit knows what kind of planet, crust, lithosphere, and geodynamic regime is supposed to produce it.

## 1. Central finding: there is no safe one-parameter planet rule

The first deep-research result is negative but important:

> WorldWright must not infer a planet's geodynamic regime, topographic amplitude, or landform style from any one variable such as mass, gravity, age, or heat.

Published modeling has reached conflicting conclusions even for the apparently simple question of whether greater rocky-planet mass makes plate tectonics more or less likely. Some models find that larger mass increases driving stress and favors plate failure; other work emphasizes stronger pressure-dependent rheology, uncertain yield behavior, surface temperature, hydration, and thermal history.

Therefore Stage 1 must treat these as separate variables:

- mass and radius;
- surface gravity;
- bulk composition and differentiation;
- internal heat budget;
- thermal age;
- mantle viscosity/rheology;
- lithosphere thickness;
- lithosphere yield strength;
- crust thickness and density;
- water/hydration;
- surface temperature;
- geodynamic regime.

They may be correlated in a scenario, but they are not synonyms.

## 2. Planetary mass, radius, density, and gravity

### 2.1 What gravity directly changes

Surface gravity changes the body force acting on rock and fluid. It affects:

- lithostatic pressure increase with depth;
- gravitational potential energy of elevated terrain;
- stress produced by topographic loads;
- the pressure gradient driving fluids and sediment downslope;
- atmospheric scale height and retention;
- the relative importance of buoyancy and material strength.

### 2.2 What gravity does not determine by itself

Gravity alone does not uniquely determine:

- maximum mountain height;
- whether plate tectonics occurs;
- whether a planet is smooth or rugged;
- whether mountains are narrow, broad, volcanic, tectonic, or impact-related;
- erosion rate;
- crust type;
- sea level or exposed land fraction.

Relief is supported by combinations of:

- crustal buoyancy and roots;
- lithosphere flexural strength;
- active tectonic stress;
- mantle flow;
- volcanic construction;
- material cohesion/friction;
- erosion and mass wasting;
- time available for relaxation.

### 2.3 Stage 1 visual implication

Gravity must be researched as a **modifier crossed with support mechanism**.

Required Stage 2 scenario axes will eventually include:

```text
low / Earth-like / high gravity
× weak / moderate / strong lithosphere
× active uplift / inactive inherited relief
× low / moderate / high erosion
```

A high-gravity world with a strong cold lithosphere may retain different relief from a high-gravity world with hot weak crust and vigorous erosion. Stage 2 must not create a single “high gravity look.”

### 2.4 Research gap

Stage 1 still requires a focused literature pass on:

- strength-limited relief scaling;
- isostatically compensated relief scaling;
- volcanic edifice stability across gravity;
- crater morphology scaling across gravity;
- fluvial and mass-wasting response to gravity.

No numerical gravity-to-relief thresholds are approved yet.

## 3. Differentiation and internal architecture

Rocky worlds differentiate into compositionally distinct layers to varying degrees. NASA's InSight mission explicitly treated core size/composition, mantle structure, crust thickness, interior temperature, heat flow, and tectonic activity as separate measurable properties. This is the correct conceptual separation for WorldWright.

### 3.1 Why differentiation matters visually

Differentiation controls or influences:

- mantle and crust composition;
- density contrasts;
- melt generation and magma chemistry;
- crust production;
- core-driven magnetic history;
- long-term heat distribution;
- buoyancy structure.

But most of these are indirect controls. A final visual audit usually cannot infer core size from one mountain range. The reference system should use differentiation to constrain scenario plausibility, not to paint a direct visible mask.

### 3.2 Stage 1 rule

Interior variables may create **obligations through intermediate processes**, never direct color or terrain stamps.

```text
interior state
→ heat / convection / melt / density structure
→ geodynamic regime and geological features
→ terrain
```

Not:

```text
core fraction → visible circular elevation field
```

## 4. Internal heat and thermal age

### 4.1 Heat sources

A rocky body's heat can include:

- primordial/accretional heat;
- radiogenic heat;
- differentiation and core-formation heat;
- tidal dissipation;
- latent heat and ongoing crystallization;
- impact heating, usually episodic and regional/global depending on event scale.

### 4.2 Heat-loss modes

Heat may escape through different combinations of:

- mobile-lid plate tectonics;
- conduction through a stagnant lid;
- mantle plumes and volcanism;
- intrusive magmatism;
- episodic overturn/resurfacing;
- heat-pipe volcanism and burial.

### 4.3 Key visual consequence

The same approximate heat budget can produce different surface patterns depending on lithosphere strength and transport mode.

Examples of distinct possible fabrics:

- **mobile lid:** globally connected linear boundary network, ridges, trenches, transforms, arcs, collisions;
- **stagnant lid:** long-lived surface shell, plume swells, volcanic provinces, localized rifting, contractional or intrusive deformation;
- **episodic lid:** long quiet intervals separated by widespread deformation/resurfacing, potentially mixed surface ages;
- **heat pipe:** intense volcanic construction and burial, thick cold lid, contraction/foundering associated with downward burial transport.

### 4.4 Age is not activity

An old planet can retain internal activity if heat sources and insulation remain sufficient. A young planet may have a magma ocean, heat-pipe behavior, distributed deformation, or an early mobile lid. Therefore:

- `planetAge` cannot substitute for `heatFlow`;
- `heatFlow` cannot substitute for `tectonicActivity`;
- `tectonicActivity` cannot substitute for a named `geodynamicRegime`.

## 5. Lithosphere thickness, strength, and rheology

### 5.1 The controlling competition

A first-order geodynamic question is whether convective and gravitational stresses can overcome lithospheric resistance.

Important controls include:

- temperature profile;
- composition and mineral phases;
- grain size;
- hydration;
- brittle friction and faulting;
- ductile creep laws;
- strain rate;
- crustal thickness;
- surface temperature;
- damage and healing history;
- inherited weak zones.

### 5.2 Why visual rules need rheology

Two planets with similar gravity and heat can differ strongly if one has:

- brittle plates separated by narrow weak boundaries;

and the other has:

- distributed weak crust or a strong unbroken lid.

This changes whether deformation appears as:

- narrow belts;
- broad plate-boundary zones;
- diffuse wrinkling;
- plume-centered radial provinces;
- episodic global resurfacing;
- almost no present-day tectonic fabric.

### 5.3 WorldWright metadata requirement

Stage 2 scenarios will need explicit labels for at least:

- lid regime;
- effective lithosphere thickness class;
- brittle vs ductile deformation balance;
- localization strength;
- inherited weakness strength;
- crustal rheology class;
- hydration state.

These need not all become user-facing sliders, but reference cases must record them.

## 6. Crust thickness, density, and buoyancy

### 6.1 Basic relationship

Crustal thickness and density help determine surface elevation through buoyancy and isostatic support. Thicker, less-dense crust generally supports higher standing continents than thin, denser crust.

### 6.2 Why simple isostasy is insufficient

Active regions may not be in local isostatic equilibrium. Topography can also be supported by:

- plate flexure;
- dynamic mantle flow;
- tectonic compression;
- elastic strength;
- thermal buoyancy;
- magmatic intrusion;
- transient loads.

Collision zones such as the Himalaya are specifically poor candidates for a purely static one-column isostatic interpretation.

### 6.3 Stage 1 visual implication

The audit must distinguish:

- broad crustally buoyant highlands;
- flexurally supported loads and adjacent basins;
- actively shortened mountain belts;
- plume/dynamic swells;
- volcanic edifices;
- impact-supported or impact-modified relief.

A high elevation value alone is not a geological diagnosis.

## 7. Gravity and interior data as hidden evidence

NASA's GRAIL mission demonstrates that gravity mapping can reveal dense structures and impact-related mass concentrations invisible in ordinary optical imagery. This is a useful methodological precedent for WorldWright:

- visible form is not the whole geological state;
- hidden density/crust/authority layers matter;
- visual interpretation should be checked against causal diagnostic layers;
- a similar shape can have different internal causes.

Stage 2 reference bundles should therefore eventually include both visible and causal layers when the process requires them.

## 8. Geodynamic regime taxonomy

## 8.1 Mobile-lid / plate tectonics

### Defining behavior

- lithosphere divided into moving plates;
- new crust generated at divergent boundaries;
- lithosphere recycled at subduction zones;
- transform motion links or offsets boundary systems;
- continental collision produces shortening and orogeny;
- deformation concentrated strongly at boundaries, with some diffuse zones.

USGS identifies divergent, convergent, transform, and broad plate-boundary zones and associates them with distinct surface processes.

### Planetary-scale visual obligations

A mature mobile-lid world should generally show a coherent network rather than isolated unrelated features:

- long ridge systems;
- trenches and arcs;
- continental margins;
- transform offsets;
- collision belts;
- crust-age organization;
- coupled creation and destruction of oceanic lithosphere.

### Valid variation

- few large plates vs many small plates;
- narrow vs diffuse boundaries;
- symmetric vs asymmetric spreading;
- oblique convergence;
- microplates and triple junctions;
- ocean-dominated vs continent-rich configurations.

### Failure signatures

- plate IDs directly visible as elevation polygons;
- all boundaries producing the same terrain shape;
- convergence, divergence, and transform zones differing only in color;
- disconnected ridges/trenches with no global plate logic;
- collision features centered on continents instead of associated with convergence geometry.

## 8.2 Stagnant-lid regime

### Defining behavior

- a strong outer shell does not participate in global mobile-plate recycling;
- interior convection and melt can continue beneath it;
- volcanism and deformation may remain active;
- horizontal tectonic organization is reduced or fundamentally different from Earth's plate network.

### Important correction

`stagnant lid` does not mean `flat`, `unchanging`, or `geologically dead`.

Possible surface expressions include:

- giant volcanic rises;
- long rift systems driven by plume/lithosphere interaction;
- coronae or distributed deformation;
- contractional scarps as a body cools;
- old cratered regions beside resurfaced volcanic plains;
- localized tectonism without a global plate mosaic.

### Stage 2 implication

Stagnant-lid worlds require their own reference families. They must not be generated by taking an Earth-like plate map and simply turning boundary intensity down.

## 8.3 Episodic-lid / overturn regimes

### Defining behavior

- long periods of limited surface mobility;
- shorter episodes of widespread lithosphere failure, overturn, or resurfacing;
- surface-age and deformation patterns potentially dominated by episodes rather than continuous boundary motion.

### Research uncertainty

Venus is a key analogue, but its resurfacing history and present tectonic style remain debated. Models include catastrophic/episodic overturn, equilibrium resurfacing, localized ongoing activity, and deformable episodic lids.

### Stage 1 rule

Stage 2 must preserve multiple episodic-lid hypothesis branches rather than producing one canonical Venus-like world.

## 8.4 Heat-pipe regime

### Defining behavior

- volcanism carries a dominant share of interior heat to the surface;
- erupted material buries older surface layers;
- burial is balanced by downward transport/foundering;
- the outer shell can remain thick and strong despite intense heat transport.

Io provides the strongest observational inspiration. Application to early Earth or other rocky planets is a hypothesis branch, not established universal history.

### Expected visual tendencies to research further

- globally abundant volcanic resurfacing;
- overlapping young lava surfaces;
- burial-dominated stratigraphy;
- thick-lid contractional mountains or blocks;
- weak or absent Earth-like plate-boundary network;
- rapid resurfacing relative to erosional maturation.

### Failure risk

A generator could mistake “high heat” for random volcano density. Heat-pipe morphology must include the burial/foundering and global heat-transport logic, not just many cones.

## 8.5 Deformable, plutonic, or squishy-lid regimes

Recent modeling explores regimes where intrusive magmatism and weak crust produce distributed deformation without Earth-like rigid plates or a perfectly immobile lid.

These regimes may create:

- broad deformation zones;
- distributed rifting and shortening;
- intrusive thickening;
- episodic surface mobility;
- complex tessera-like terrain;
- volcanic-tectonic provinces with no simple plate boundaries.

This category is currently model-dependent and needs a dedicated source pass before Stage 2.

## 8.6 Hemispheric tectonics

Models for strongly irradiated, tidally locked rocky exoplanets show that extreme day–night thermal contrast could organize mantle upwellings and downwellings hemispherically.

This is not an observed mapped surface regime, but it is a useful warning against assuming rotationally symmetric or Earth-like convection on all worlds.

Stage 2 inclusion decision remains open pending:

- model robustness review;
- relevance to WorldWright's intended planet classes;
- ability to define visible consequences without fabricating certainty.

## 9. First interaction matrix

| Interaction | Why it matters | Unsafe simplification | Research obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| gravity × lithosphere strength | Controls load stress and support | high gravity = flat | model multiple strength branches |
| gravity × erosion | Alters transport potential and slope stability | low gravity = giant mountains | separate construction, support, and degradation |
| heat × lithosphere strength | Determines whether heat produces plates, plumes, or overturn | hot = tectonic plates | branch by regime |
| age × heat production | Old worlds can remain active; young worlds can cool quickly depending on size/composition | old = dead | explicit heat history |
| water × rheology | Hydration may weaken rocks and faults | wet = guaranteed plate tectonics | record uncertainty and model dependence |
| crust thickness × density | Controls buoyancy and roots | thick = high everywhere | include composition/density and active support |
| surface temperature × rheology | Hot surfaces may weaken or heal lithosphere differently | hot = more deformation | regime-specific modeling required |
| volcanism × lid thickness | Controls eruption distribution and intrusive/extrusive balance | more heat = more cones | research magma transport mode |
| tidal heating × shell mechanics | Can sustain extreme volcanism and fracture patterns | tidal = random global volcanism | use Io/icy-moon analogs carefully |

## 10. Initial WorldWright audit obligations

The research already supports several non-numeric architectural requirements.

### 10.1 Required explicit scenario fields

A reference scenario should not be considered fully labeled without:

- gravity;
- thermal age;
- current heat-flow class;
- geodynamic regime;
- lithosphere strength/thickness class;
- crust thickness/density class;
- hydration state;
- dominant heat-loss mode;
- activity state: active, episodic, waning, fossil.

### 10.2 Required causal separation

The audit should be able to distinguish:

```text
planetary parameter
geodynamic regime
geological cause field
terrain response
surface modification
final rendering
```

### 10.3 No direct masks

None of these hidden parameters should be directly recognizable as raw circular, polygonal, or distance-field shapes in final terrain.

## 11. Stage 2 coverage requirements generated by this module

These are requirements for the future coverage matrix, not permission to generate images yet.

### Foundation sweeps

- low / Earth-like / high gravity;
- thin / moderate / thick crust;
- low / moderate / high density contrast;
- cold-strong / intermediate / hot-weak lithosphere;
- low / moderate / high internal heat;
- dry / hydrated crust and mantle branches;
- young-active / mature / old-fossil states.

### Regime families

- mature mobile lid;
- diffuse mobile lid;
- stagnant lid with low volcanism;
- stagnant lid with plume provinces;
- episodic resurfacing branches;
- deformable episodic/plutonic lid;
- heat-pipe end member;
- optional hemispheric regime if later approved.

### Required negative controls

- same parameter labels but wrong regime morphology;
- raw circular heat mask visible in topography;
- Earth plate network incorrectly applied to stagnant-lid world;
- random volcano scatter presented as heat-pipe tectonics;
- crust-thickness field directly painted as final elevation;
- age directly mapped to smoothing without causal processes.

## 13. Current conclusions safe enough to carry forward

### High confidence

- plate boundaries on an Earth-like mobile-lid world form distinct causal families;
- crust, mantle, heat, and tectonic activity must be separate state variables;
- hidden interior/density structure can matter even when invisible optically;
- stagnant-lid worlds can remain volcanically and tectonically active;
- crustal thickness and density influence elevation but do not uniquely determine it;
- active topography can depart from simple isostatic equilibrium;
- geodynamic regime must condition all later terrain expectations.

### Moderate confidence / model-dependent

- increasing rocky-planet mass may favor or inhibit plate tectonics depending on rheology and assumptions;
- Venus may occupy stagnant, episodic, deformable episodic, or mixed regimes;
- intrusive magmatism may create non-plate distributed-lid regimes;
- heat-pipe behavior may have been important on early rocky planets beyond Io.

### Not approved

- universal gravity-to-mountain-height equation for Stage 2;
- deterministic mass-to-plate-tectonics rule;
- universal age-to-activity rule;
- one canonical stagnant-lid appearance;
- one canonical Venus history;
- any generated reference image.
